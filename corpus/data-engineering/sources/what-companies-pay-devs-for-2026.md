# Source: What Companies Really Pay Devs For in 2026

**Author**: Tech With Tim (tim@techwithtim.net)
**Received**: 2026-06-30 via email/Gmail

## Summary

Short newsletter piece arguing that AI's ability to generate code does not threaten developers who understand the real job: problem understanding, trade-offs, design, and systems thinking. These are the durable, paid-for skills.

## Key claims

- "Writing code was never the hardest part of software engineering."[^src]
- The real work: understanding problems, breaking them down, thinking through trade-offs, designing solutions, working with managers and designers, clarifying requirements, reviewing decisions.[^src]
- The word "engineering" in "software engineering" carries the judgment and design burden that code generation can't replace.[^src]
- Skills companies actually pay for (not syntax memorization): breaking down vague problems; understanding why one solution > another; spotting fragile AI-generated code; explaining trade-offs; debugging rather than delegating to AI.[^src]
- Risk of over-relying on AI: "If you ask AI for the answer every time you get stuck, you might still get working code, but you miss the struggle that builds real understanding."[^src]
- "Companies do not pay developers to just produce code. They pay developers to think through problems, make good decisions, and build software that actually works in the real world."[^src]
