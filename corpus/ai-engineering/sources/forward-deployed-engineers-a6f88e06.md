# Forward Deployed Engineers and the Future of Software Engineering (Latent Space, 2026-07-01)

**Source**: latent.space — `raw/_inbox/web-forward-deployed-engineers-and-the-future-of-software-engine-a6f88e06.md`

## Summary

Q&A with Natalie Meurer (Head of Agent Engineering at Sierra), presented at the AI Engineer World's Fair 2026, on the convergence of forward deployed engineering (FDE) and product engineering via AI agents. [^1]

## Core argument

FDE is defined more by **accountability to customers** than by a specific skill set. The range of associated skill sets has become so broad that the title "forward deployed engineer" can be almost nonsensical. [^1]

## Sierra's "agent engineer" framing

Sierra calls the role "agent engineer" rather than "forward deployed engineer" because the title should capture the shape of the technical work, not just the customer-obsession element. Agent engineering combines: systems integration, agent development, customer operations understanding, product sense, and end-user UX design. [^1]

## What the work looks like at Sierra

- Sierra builds conversational AI agents for inbound/outbound customer service: voice, chat, email channels [^1]
- Discovery phase: find intersection of "genuinely difficult problems" + "meaningful business impact" [^1]
- Most customer-specific work happens at the **orchestration layer**, not the model layer [^1]
- Sierra uses an "orchestrated constellation of models" internally (best tool for each job) [^1]
- Sierra reaches production in 40–60 days per enterprise customer [^1]
- Each agent is customized around the customer's APIs, systems, SOPs, brand, and tone [^1]

## Key insight: convergence of FDE and product engineering

> "When code becomes cheap to author, it also becomes easier to translate customer insights directly into a product. Product engineering and forward deployed engineering are therefore converging." [^1]

- Best product engineers should be talking to customers
- Best FDEs should be building the product
- "I think that is new." — Meurer [^1]

## On enterprise change management

Enterprises need hundreds or thousands of contributors to agentic systems, each following a discrete release process — a change-management problem that may be harder than the technical implementation. [^1]

## On generalist vs. specialist developers

Meurer leans toward generalists becoming more valuable, not specialists: "Forward deployed engineering has historically been the classic generalist role because it combines engineering with customer-facing work." [^1]
