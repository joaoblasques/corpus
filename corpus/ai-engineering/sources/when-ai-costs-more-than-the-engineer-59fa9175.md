# When AI Costs More Than the Engineer (Tom Tunguz, 2026-06-30)

**Source**: tomtunguz.com — `raw/_inbox/web-when-ai-costs-more-than-the-engineer-59fa9175.md`

## Summary

Tom Tunguz analyzed AI infrastructure spend relative to engineering payroll using Ramp AI Index data (June 2026), finding a 680x gap between frontier AI-native firms and the median company. [^1]

## Key data points

**Anthropic internal**: Spends 2.3x its payroll on compute — approximately $2M compute per employee per year vs. ~$500K all-in compensation. With ~5,000 employees and ~$10B inference/training spend in 2026. [^1]

**Top 1% of companies (Ramp AI Index, June 2026)**: $89K/engineer/year on AI — 40% of a fully-loaded $224K senior engineer salary. Growing 14.1% month-over-month. [^1]

**Median firm**: $137/year on AI per employee. [^1]

**680x spending gap** between top-1% and median companies. [^1]

**Revenue per employee**: Anthropic ~$14M, OpenAI ~$6.5M — highest in the Forbes Global 2000. [^1]

## Three scenarios for 2026–2029 AI spend per engineer

(As % of $224K fully-loaded senior engineer salary) [^1]

| Year | Bear | Base | Bull |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 | $90K (40%) | $90K (40%) | $90K (40%) |
| 2027 | $106K (45%) | $164K (70%) | $258K (110%) |
| 2028 | $118K (48%) | $259K (105%) | $444K (180%) |
| 2029 | $106K (41%) | $363K (140%) | $596K (230%) |

**Bull drivers**: Frontier model prices hold as training costs plateau; agentic workloads drive 24x token consumption increase by 2030 (Goldman Sachs projection); competitive pressure makes AI spend mandatory. [^1]

**Bear counterweights**: Token prices have fallen ~10x/year for three years; open-weight models close quality gap at fraction of proprietary API cost; companies ration usage by role. [^1]
