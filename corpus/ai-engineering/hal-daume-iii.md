# Hal Daumé III

**TL;DR:** Author of *A Course in Machine Learning* (2015, open access), a pedagogically sequenced ML textbook aimed at undergraduates and early graduate students; web presence at hal3.name/courseml/.

## Identity

Hal Daumé III is the author of *A Course in Machine Learning* (copyright 2015). The book is available online at `http://hal3.name/courseml/` along with associated code, data, and errata. [^ciml-p01]

## Textbook: *A Course in Machine Learning*

The book's stated purpose is to provide "a _gentle_ and _pedagogically organized_ introduction to the field," explicitly contrasting with most ML texts that organize content topically rather than for learners. [^ciml-p01]

A secondary goal is to focus on "ideas and models, not on math" — math is present to aid understanding, not hinder it. [^ciml-p01]

The book is designed for minimal chapter dependencies, allowing flexible chapter selection; where dependencies exist they are stated at chapter start. [^ciml-p01]

**Target audience:** readers who know differential calculus, discrete math, and can program reasonably well. Described as suitable for "an undergraduate in their fourth or fifth semester" or first-year graduate students at a faster pace. [^ciml-p01]

**Course fit:** structured for a single-semester undergraduate course, a graduate course, or a two-semester course covering the full book. [^ciml-p01]

### Chapter coverage (193 pages, part 1 of 10)

Topics across the full text include: Decision Trees, Geometry and Nearest Neighbors, The Perceptron, Practical Issues, Beyond Binary Classification, Linear Models, Probabilistic Modeling, Neural Networks, Kernel Methods, Learning Theory, Ensemble Methods, Efficient Learning, Unsupervised Learning, Expectation Maximization, Semi-Supervised Learning, Graphical Models, Online Learning, Structured Learning Tasks, and Bayesian Learning. [^ciml-p01]

### Pedagogical approach

The book frames learning as predicting the future based on the past, opening with the concrete decision-tree model to establish what "learning" means before progressively loosening and adapting that framing. [^ciml-p01]

Key concepts introduced early: inductive bias, the distinction between memorization and generalization, regularization as a trade-off between underfitting and overfitting, and proper use of test data. [^ciml-p01]
