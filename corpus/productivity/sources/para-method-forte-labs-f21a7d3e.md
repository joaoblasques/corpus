# The PARA Method: Simple System for Organizing Digital Information

**TL;DR** — PARA is Tiago Forte's four-category system for organizing all digital information: Projects (active, goal-directed, finite), Areas (ongoing responsibilities), Resources (reference by topic), Archives (inactive items from the other three). The key organizing principle: **actionability**, not subject — sort by what you're trying to accomplish right now, not by academic-style categories.[^src1]

## The four categories

"There are only four categories that encompass all the information in your life."[^src1]

| Category | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Projects** | Short-term efforts with a specific goal and end-state | "Renovate bathroom"; "Write research report"; "Complete webpage design" |
| **Areas** | Ongoing responsibilities that require sustained attention | Health, Finances, Marketing, Direct Reports |
| **Resources** | Topics of interest for future reference | Graphic design, Coffee, Photography |
| **Archives** | Inactive items from the other three | Completed projects, old areas, dropped interests |

The system uses four top-level folders containing subfolders for each active project, area, resource, and archive.

## The core insight: organize by actionability

"Most of us first learned how to organize information in school. We were taught to categorize our class notes, handouts, and study material by academic subject… But then without realizing it, we took that same approach into adulthood."[^src1]

The problem: broad subject categories ("Psychology", "Business") are not actionable. You cannot quickly surface the specific material you need for a current decision.

PARA's reframe: "organize it according to the projects and goals you are committed to right now." When you sit down to work on a project, all related material is in one place.[^src1]

## Why projects (not areas) are the critical distinction

Knowing whether something is a project (finite, completable) vs. an area (ongoing) matters for two reasons:[^src1]

1. **Commitments visibility**: an area like "Hiring" could mean 1 hire per 6 months or 50 hires this quarter — you can't tell. Breaking it into named projects makes scope legible.
2. **Goal connection**: areas never end, so measuring yourself only against areas creates "an endless horizon that never gets any closer." Projects complete; completing them creates "a cadence of regular victories."

## Complexity floor

"If your organizational system is as complex as your life, then the demands of maintaining it will end up robbing you of the time and energy you need to live that life."[^src1]

PARA deliberately offers no further complexity. Four folders, radically simple, applied across all platforms (file system, cloud storage, notes app).
