# A Cool Brisk Walk Through Discrete Mathematics (Davies, v2.2.2)

TL;DR: A concise, CS-practitioner-oriented tour of discrete mathematics for undergraduate CS students. 254 pages, 9 chapters. Emphasizes intuition and fluency over theorem proving. CC BY-SA 4.0. All 9 parts ingested.

**Author**: Stephen Davies, Ph.D., University of Mary Washington, Department of Computer Science.[^1]

**Access**: Free at [allthemath.org](http://www.allthemath.org); LaTeX source on GitHub.[^1]

## Key themes

- Math deals with **abstract conceptual objects and their properties** — not just numbers.[^1]
- **Discrete vs. continuous**: discrete things jump between crisp positions (integers, algorithms, digital); continuous things vary smoothly (reals, calculus, analog).[^1]
- Σ (summation) is the discrete analogue of ∫ (integration); subtraction is the discrete analogue of differentiation.[^1]
- Computers are inherently discrete: bits, steps, counts — so CS benefits from discrete thinking more than continuous math.[^1]
- Sets are not arrays: **no order, no duplicates, possibly infinite, untyped**.[^1]

## Pedagogical approach

"A cool, brisk walk through a pretty forest" — breadth over depth. Target audience is CS practitioners who need fluency with these tools, not mathematicians who need to derive them. Exercise format: index-card flashcard style (left column: question; right column: answer).[^1]
